# Research Quality Standards

## Executive Summary

This document establishes the official quality standards for every research artifact produced within ForgeOS.

Its purpose is to ensure that every research output is trustworthy, reproducible, evidence-based, and actionable.

Quality is measured by the reliability of knowledge—not by the volume of documentation.

---

# Purpose

Research exists to reduce uncertainty.

Poor-quality research increases uncertainty.

Therefore, every research deliverable shall comply with these standards before entering organizational knowledge.

---

# Scope

These standards apply to:

- Market Research
- Competitor Analysis
- User Research
- Technology Research
- Industry Research
- AI Research
- Business Research
- Strategic Research
- Innovation Research

---

# Quality Principles

Every research artifact shall satisfy the following principles.

## Principle 01 — Objectivity

Research shall remain impartial.

Evidence has priority over opinion.

Bias shall be explicitly identified whenever detected.

---

## Principle 02 — Verifiability

Every important claim shall be verifiable.

Research must provide sufficient references to allow independent validation.

---

## Principle 03 — Traceability

Every conclusion must be traceable back to:

- Sources
- Data
- Interviews
- Benchmarks
- Reports
- Experiments

No unsupported conclusions are permitted.

---

## Principle 04 — Completeness

Research shall answer the original research objective.

Missing information shall be documented rather than ignored.

---

## Principle 05 — Actionability

Research must enable decision-making.

Every report should conclude with clear recommendations.

---

## Principle 06 — Reusability

Research is an organizational asset.

Documentation shall be written so future projects can reuse its findings.

---

# Source Quality Levels

ForgeOS classifies research sources according to reliability.

| Level | Source Type | Reliability |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| A | Official Documentation | Very High |
| A | Government Publications | Very High |
| A | Academic Papers | Very High |
| B | Vendor Documentation | High |
| B | Industry Reports | High |
| B | Public APIs | High |
| C | Professional Blogs | Medium |
| C | Conference Talks | Medium |
| D | Community Discussions | Low |
| D | Social Media | Low |

Whenever possible, Level A and Level B sources shall be prioritized.

---

# Evidence Standards

Every significant finding shall include evidence.

Evidence may include:

- Statistics
- Benchmarks
- User Interviews
- Usage Metrics
- Industry Reports
- Public Documentation
- Experiments
- Internal Analytics

Claims without evidence shall be clearly identified as hypotheses.

---

# Research Validation Checklist

Every report shall validate:

☐ Research objective achieved

☐ Findings supported by evidence

☐ Sources documented

☐ Risks identified

☐ Recommendations included

☐ Assumptions separated from facts

☐ Documentation complete

☐ References verified

---

# Quality Metrics

Research quality is evaluated using:

## Accuracy

Are findings correct?

---

## Credibility

Are sources trustworthy?

---

## Coverage

Does the report sufficiently cover the topic?

---

## Clarity

Can another team understand the findings?

---

## Consistency

Does the report align with organizational standards?

---

## Decision Support

Can leadership make informed decisions from this report?

---

# Review Process

Every research document follows the same review process.

```
Research Complete

↓

Self Review

↓

Peer Review

↓

Department Review

↓

Quality Review

↓

Approval

↓

Knowledge Base
```

No report bypasses Quality Review.

---

# Research Quality Score

Every report receives a quality score.

| Score | Status |
|--------|--------|
| 95–100 | Excellent |
| 90–94 | Approved |
| 80–89 | Minor Revision |
| 70–79 | Major Revision |
| Below 70 | Rejected |

Only documents scoring **90 or above** may enter the organizational knowledge base.

---

# Common Quality Issues

Research should avoid:

- Unsupported conclusions
- Outdated information
- Missing references
- Confirmation bias
- Incomplete analysis
- Poor documentation
- Duplicate work
- Ambiguous recommendations
