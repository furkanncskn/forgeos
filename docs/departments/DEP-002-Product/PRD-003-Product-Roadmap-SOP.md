# Product Roadmap Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## Executive Summary

This SOP defines how ForgeOS creates, manages, maintains, and evolves Product Roadmaps.

The Product Roadmap translates an approved PRD into a strategic delivery plan.

It aligns every department around one shared execution timeline.

A roadmap is not a task list.

A roadmap is a strategic commitment.

---

# Purpose

The Product Roadmap exists to answer:

- What will be delivered?
- Why will it be delivered?
- When should it be delivered?
- In which order?
- Which business outcome does each milestone create?

---

# Scope

This SOP applies to:

- New Products
- SaaS Platforms
- Mobile Applications
- AI Products
- Enterprise Systems
- Platform Evolution
- Major Product Releases

---

# Inputs

Roadmap Planning begins after:

- Product Discovery Approved
- PRD Approved
- Executive Approval
- Architecture Feasibility Confirmed

---

# Outputs

The Roadmap process produces:

- Product Roadmap
- Release Timeline
- Milestone Plan
- Dependency Map
- Feature Prioritization
- Delivery Strategy
- Executive Roadmap Summary

---

# Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| Chief Product Officer | Final Approval |
| Senior Product Manager | Roadmap Owner |
| Product Manager | Planning |
| Architecture Office | Technical Dependencies |
| Engineering Lead | Delivery Estimation |
| Marketing Lead | Launch Planning |

# Roadmap Principles

Every roadmap shall satisfy the following principles.

## Principle 01 — Outcome Driven

Roadmaps communicate outcomes.

Not task lists.

---

## Principle 02 — Customer Value First

Highest customer value receives highest priority.

---

## Principle 03 — Business Alignment

Every milestone supports a measurable business objective.

---

## Principle 04 — Flexibility

Roadmaps evolve.

Strategy remains stable.

---

## Principle 05 — Transparency

Roadmaps remain visible across ForgeOS.

---

# Workflow

## Phase 1 — Strategic Alignment

### Objective

Align roadmap with company strategy.

Evaluate:

- Mission
- Vision
- Product Strategy
- Business Objectives

### Deliverable

Strategic Alignment Report

---

## Phase 2 — Feature Prioritization

### Objective

Prioritize work.

Evaluate:

- Customer Value
- Business Impact
- Technical Complexity
- Risk
- Strategic Importance

Recommended frameworks:

- RICE
- MoSCoW
- WSJF

### Deliverable

Prioritized Feature List

---

## Phase 3 — Dependency Mapping

### Objective

Identify delivery dependencies.

Document:

- Technical Dependencies
- Business Dependencies
- External Vendors
- APIs
- Legal Constraints

### Deliverable

Dependency Map

---

## Phase 4 — Release Planning

### Objective

Group features into releases.

Possible releases:

- MVP
- Beta
- General Availability
- Enterprise
- Future Releases

### Deliverable

Release Plan

---

## Phase 5 — Milestone Planning

### Objective

Define measurable milestones.

Each milestone shall include:

- Objective
- Deliverables
- Success Metrics
- Exit Criteria

### Deliverable

Milestone Plan

---

## Phase 6 — Resource Alignment

### Objective

Verify execution capability.

Coordinate with:

- Design
- Architecture
- Engineering
- QA
- DevOps
- Marketing

### Deliverable

Capacity Assessment

---

## Phase 7 — Timeline Creation

### Objective

Create strategic delivery timeline.

Timeline should include:

- Discovery
- Design
- Architecture
- Development
- QA
- Beta
- Launch
- Growth

### Deliverable

Roadmap Timeline

---

## Phase 8 — Executive Review

### Objective

Validate roadmap.

Review:

- Business Goals
- Delivery Risks
- Dependencies
- Budget
- Capacity

### Deliverable

Executive Review Report

---

## Phase 9 — Publication

### Objective

Publish official roadmap.

Roadmap becomes visible to:

- Executive Office
- Product
- Design
- Engineering
- QA
- Marketing
- Growth

### Deliverable

Official Product Roadmap

---

## Phase 10 — Continuous Review

### Objective

Keep roadmap current.

Review cadence:

- Sprint
- Monthly
- Quarterly
- Major Release

Approved changes require roadmap version updates.

---

# Roadmap Structure

Every roadmap shall include:

- Vision
- Strategic Goals
- Product Themes
- Releases
- Milestones
- Features
- Dependencies
- Timeline
- Risks
- Success Metrics

---

# Mandatory Questions

Every roadmap must answer:

1. Why are we building this?
2. Which business objective is supported?
3. Which customer value is delivered?
4. What is the release order?
5. What are the dependencies?
6. Which teams are involved?
7. Which risks exist?
8. How will success be measured?
9. When is delivery expected?
10. What happens after launch?

---

# Success Criteria

Roadmap succeeds when:

- Teams share one execution plan.
- Priorities remain stable.
- Delivery becomes predictable.
- Stakeholders remain aligned.
- Customer value is delivered continuously.

---

# KPIs

Roadmap performance is measured through:

- Roadmap Predictability
- Milestone Completion Rate
- Release Success Rate
- Feature Adoption
- Executive Satisfaction
- Planning Accuracy

---

# Exit Criteria

Roadmap is complete when:

- Executive approval received
- Official roadmap published
- Departments aligned
- Delivery planning begins

---

# Architect Notes

A roadmap communicates strategic intent.

Sprint planning belongs to Engineering.

Implementation scheduling belongs to Delivery Management.

These responsibilities remain intentionally separate.

---

# Implementation Notes

Every ForgeOS product shall maintain exactly one active Product Roadmap.

Archived roadmaps remain part of organizational knowledge.

---

# AI Consumption Notes

AI Professionals shall use the roadmap to understand strategic priorities.

Implementation recommendations should always align with roadmap priorities unless formally overridden.
